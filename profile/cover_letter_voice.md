# Cover letter voice & style guide

Notes extracted from a past cover letter Sergey was happy with (AbaData application).
`/apply` should match this voice rather than inventing a new one each time.

## Structure (5 paragraphs)
1. **Opening** — role + company name, one line on years of experience, one line on
   genuine interest in what the company does (specific, not generic flattery).
2. **Track record** — 2-3 sentences summarizing the breadth of relevant experience
   (test planning, manual + automated testing, defect management, cross-functional
   collaboration), pitched at the level of the target role.
3. **Why this company** — the one paragraph that MUST change per application. Name a
   specific product, technology, or mission detail from the job posting/company site
   and connect it to what Sergey enjoys working on. This is where fabrication risk is
   highest — only reference things confirmed in the job posting or company site.
4. **Personal working style** — analytical mindset, attention to detail, clear
   communication, reproducible defect reports, enjoys learning new tech. If the
   posting emphasizes automation, API/security testing, or AI-assisted QA, this
   is the spot to mention ONE relevant project from `profile/profile.yaml`'s
   `portfolio` list with its URL — one short clause tying it to what the
   posting asks for, not a project description dump. Only include it when it
   genuinely strengthens the fit; skip it for postings where it wouldn't land
   (e.g. pure manual QA roles). Never include both portfolio links in the same
   letter — pick whichever is more relevant.
5. **Closing** — thank them, express willingness to discuss further, sign "Sincerely,"
   + full name.

## Tone
- Confident but not boastful. States facts ("15+ years") rather than superlatives
  ("world-class").
- Warm and direct, short sentences, no corporate jargon.
- Willing to admit being new to a specific tool/domain while framing it as eagerness
  to learn — do this only when true for the role in question.
- Never use em dashes or overly formal phrasing ("herein", "aforementioned").

## Length
4-6 short paragraphs, should fit on one page in the Word template.
